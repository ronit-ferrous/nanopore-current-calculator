Nanopore Current Calculator
Literature-based open-pore conductance estimates

Estimate ionic current from pore geometry, electrolyte conductivity, and bias.

This app implements the standard cylindrical nanopore model with access resistance. It is useful for quick theoretical estimates of open-pore conductance and current before comparing to measured data.

Model used

For a cylindrical pore of diameter d, length L, solution conductivity σ, and applied voltage V:

Rpore = 4L / (σπd²)
Raccess,total = 1 / (σd)

G = 1 / (Rpore + Raccess,total)
I = G × V

This is algebraically equivalent to the commonly used expression based on channel resistance plus two access-resistance terms for a circular nanopore.

How to use it

  • Enter pore diameter, membrane thickness, and bias voltage.
  • Select 1× PBS or enter your own conductivity.
  • Use the current estimate as an open-pore benchmark, then compare it to measured values.
  • If measured current is much lower, possible causes include a smaller effective pore, partial blockage, non-cylindrical geometry, or incorrect gain/scaling in the electronics.

Limitations

  • This is an ohmic estimate for symmetric electrolyte conditions and an open pore.
  • Surface charge, concentration polarization, double-layer overlap, and non-cylindrical shapes can shift real conductance.
  • The simple model is strongest as a first-pass estimate, not as a full inverse reconstruction of exact pore shape.

Literature basis

Access resistance is part of total nanopore resistance

Hyun et al. describe total resistance as the sum of pore resistance and access resistance, with one-sided access resistance given by ρ / 4a, leading to the familiar total resistance form for a cylindrical pore.

Conductance depends on geometry and surface chemistry

Frament and Dwyer show that cylindrical, conical, and conical-cylindrical models can reproduce conductance differently, so geometry assumptions matter. That is why this app exposes a simple shape factor and states that the cylinder model is a first approximation.

Read article

Why measured current may differ

Real pores can have effective lengths and shapes that differ from nominal membrane thickness, especially in thin or sculpted membranes. The app therefore reports theoretical values, which should be compared with experiment as a benchmark rather than treated as exact truth.
